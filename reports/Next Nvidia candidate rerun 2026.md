# Credo Survives Its Bear Case, Narrowly

*Research report, September 28, 2026. This is research, not investment advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security; the author has no position information and the reader should assume every stock discussed can fall 50% or more. All figures are as of the dates cited.*

**Bottom line up front.** Credo Technology (CRDO) is still the publicly traded stock with the strongest setup to "blow up like Nvidia" as of September 28, 2026, but the setup is materially thinner than it was on September 8. The stock closed at **$210.97 on September 25** (market cap roughly **$37 to 39B**), up 24% from $170.57 on September 7 after a nine-session, 41% streak from a mid-September low near $150, and that move happened on conference optics, analyst target resets and a broad semiconductor rebound rather than on any new customer, design win or guidance change ([Trefis, Sept 26](https://www.trefis.com/stock/crdo/articles/616737/a-9-day-winning-streak-has-credo-technology-stock-up-41/2026-09-26); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-credo-technology-stock-rallying-today-93CH-4909330)). A deliberate attempt to break the Credo thesis found real mechanisms (Marvell's "Golden Cable" program with Amphenol, Luxshare and TE; March 2026 patent settlements that license rivals into active electrical cables; GAAP gross margin down from 68.2% to 64.5% in one quarter; top-three customers at 74% of revenue; a fiscal-2027 guide that requires roughly $700M of quarterly revenue in the second half against $479M in the first quarter) but no evidence of a lost hyperscaler socket, no published short report, and short interest of only 3.7% of float ([Semi Fundamental](https://semifundamental.substack.com/p/active-electrical-cable-aec-fundamentals); [Motley Fool, Sept 2](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/02/why-credo-technology-stock-plunged-20-today/); [Equibles](https://equibles.com/stocks/crdo/short-interest)). The September 8 ranking (Credo first, Lumentum second, Astera Labs third) survives, but with two changes: Lumentum is now effectively tied with Credo on the scorecard and is the purer "dominant supplier of a binding constraint" name, losing the tie only on market-cap headroom; and the margin of safety that made Credo a high-conviction pick three weeks ago has been partly consumed by the rally. The backdrop turned harder, not easier: the Fed hiked 25 basis points on September 16 to 3.75 to 4.00%, the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.13%, the semiconductor index fell 5.9% in one day on September 14, Oracle posted a $664B backlog alongside negative $5.4B free cash flow, and Goldman raised 2027 hyperscaler capex to $1.2T ([CNBC, Sept 16](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html); [CNN, Sept 14](https://us.cnn.com/2026/09/14/business/ai-stocks-slide-slowdown-development-amodei-altman-intl); [Oracle](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/q1fy27-earnings-release-2026-09-10/); [Bloomberg, Sept 25](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/goldman-sees-hyperscaler-ai-capex-rising-50-to-1-2-trillion)). Micron reports on September 30 and its number is not known. The historical base rate is sobering: after a 100% two-year run-up, more than half of industries crash within two years, and every named 2000-era optical and networking supplier with sourced data fell 88 to 99.8% when that cycle turned ([NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23191); [CNBC on Cisco](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/10/ciscos-stock-closes-at-record-for-first-time-since-dot-com-peak-2000.html)).

Three prices were confirmed directly by the research coordinator and override anything inferred in the underlying notes: Credo $210.97 (Sept 25), SiTime $643 to 662 (Sept 25, sources differ), and Nvidia roughly $222 to 225 at about $5.5T. | Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| Market cap | Share price times shares outstanding: what the whole company is valued at. A 10x needs the cap to grow tenfold. |
| Forward P/E | Price divided by next year's expected earnings per share. Lower means the market is paying less for each dollar of expected profit. |
| Gross margin | Revenue minus the cost of making the product, as a percentage. Above 60% usually means the company sells intellectual property, not commodities. |
| Hyperscaler | The biggest cloud builders: Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle. Their capital spending ("capex") is the demand for everything below. |
| Binding constraint | The one input the whole buildout cannot proceed without. Whoever sells it gets pricing power. GPUs in 2023, memory in 2025 to 2026. |
| HBM | High-bandwidth memory, stacked DRAM chips bolted next to an AI processor. Sold out through 2027. |
| AEC | Active electrical cable: a copper cable with a small chip inside that lets it carry 800G or 1.6T signals a few meters. Credo invented the category. |
| EML | Electro-absorption modulated laser: the laser chip inside a high-speed optical transceiver. Lumentum holds 50 to 60% share at the newest speed grade. |
| CPO | Co-packaged optics: putting the laser and optics directly on the switch chip instead of in a pluggable module. A threat to some component makers, a gift to laser makers. |
| RPO / backlog | Remaining performance obligations: contracted revenue not yet delivered. Big backlogs with negative cash flow are a warning sign, not just a bullish one. |
| Beta | How much a stock moves relative to the market. Beta 3 means a 10% market drop tends to become a 30% drop in the stock. |
| Book-to-bill | Orders divided by shipments. Above 1 means demand is running ahead of supply. |

## Nvidia's run was earnings outrunning price, and history says suppliers crash 70 to 99% when cycles turn

Nvidia's move from the October 2022 low is roughly **16 to 20x in market cap** ($336.8B at the end of October 2022 to about $5.4 to 5.5T in September 2026), and the mechanism matters more than the magnitude: earnings grew faster than the stock, so the forward P/E fell from 52x in June 2023 to roughly 17 to 25x in 2026 while the price went vertical ([StatMuse](https://www.statmuse.com/money/ask/nvidia-market-cap-in-october-2022); [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/nvidias-p-e-ratio-current-levels-historical-trends-and-outlook); [Motley Fool, Jul 11 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/11/nvidias-forward-pe-has-actually-fallen-as-its-stoc/)). Revenue went from $26.97B and flat in fiscal 2023 to $60.9B (+126%) in fiscal 2024, $215.9B in fiscal 2026 and $96.2B (+106%) in the single quarter reported August 26, 2026, with GAAP gross margin rising from about 57% to 75% ([SEC 8-K Q4 FY23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581023000014/q4fy23pr.htm); [SEC 8-K Q2 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm)). Five things were visible in advance: Nvidia was the sole supplier of the binding constraint (accelerators, with CUDA as the lock); supply could not respond for two years (CoWoS packaging, then HBM), which is what turned a 57% gross margin into 75%; its customers' capex plans were public and rising; consensus lagged guidance for eight-plus quarters (the May 2023 guide of $11B against roughly $7.2B expected was the first of many); and the starting base was small enough, at sub-$300B, for a 20x. Any candidate has to be tested against all five, and the fifth is where most 2026 names fail.

Four cycles of "picks and shovels" winners show a consistent starting profile. Every sourced 10x-plus winner other than Nvidia in 2022 started under about $20B in market cap. Growth at the start was often unremarkable (Nvidia fiscal 2016 revenue +7%; AMD 2016 +7% with a 23% gross margin and a net loss; Vertiv 2023 +21%; Super Micro's December 2022 quarter +54%) but inflected to triple digits within two to four quarters ([Nvidia 10-K FY2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581016000205/nvda-2016x10k.htm); [AMD 10-K FY2016](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000002488/000000248817000043/amd-12312016x10k.htm); [Vertiv 8-K, Feb 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1674101/000119312524115471/d783439dars.pdf); [Supermicro Q2 FY23](https://www.supermicro.com/en/pressreleases/supermicro-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-year-2023-financial-results)). Starting gross margin did not discriminate (18.7% at Super Micro, about 10% at Celestica, 66 to 74% at Arista, Credo and Astera), but margin expansion during the run did, and the low-margin winners were the ones whose runs ended fastest. Customer concentration was the norm at the start (Arista had roughly 40% of sales from Microsoft and Meta; Credo had 67% from Amazon in fiscal 2025) and became the failure channel at the end ([Arista 10-K FY2014](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001596532/000159653215000004/anet20141231-10k.htm)). The single most consistent signature was that the company sold the binding constraint of its buildout: fiber and lasers in 2000, GPUs in 2023, HBM and DRAM in 2025 to 2026, EML lasers in 2026.

The end of the last comparable cycle is the base rate every candidate has to be weighed against. Cisco peaked at $80.06 and a $579B market cap on March 27, 2000 at a P/E of 201 and bottomed at $8.60 in October 2002, an 89% decline that took until December 2025 to recover ([CNBC, Dec 10 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/10/ciscos-stock-closes-at-record-for-first-time-since-dot-com-peak-2000.html); [wccftech](https://wccftech.com/24-years-ago-cisco-beat-microsoft-became-worlds-most-valuable-company/)). JDS Uniphase, whose fiscal 2000 revenue grew 406% (mostly by acquisition), fell 99.8%; Corning, whose fiber sales grew about 70% in 2000, fell 99.0% from $113.33 on September 1, 2000 to $1.10 in October 2002; EMC fell 96.5% ([JDSU 10-K/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000912093/000109581101001202/f69300e10-ka.txt); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/forbes/2011/0314/technology-eric-savitz-tech-trade-light-returns.html); [ISE Magazine](https://www.isemag.com/professional-development-leadership/article/55293922/the-perils-of-irrational-exuberancethe-25th-anniversary-of-the-dot-com-boom); [Cabot Wealth](https://www.cabotwealth.com/daily/stock-market/emc-corporation)). The pattern repeated in miniature twice since: Super Micro peaked at $1,188 (about $70B) in March 2024 and was down 80% by November 2024, eight months later, after a short-seller report and a delayed 10-K; Nvidia's own 2018 crypto overhang turned a $3.4B expected quarter into a $2.2B one in ten weeks and made it the worst S&P 500 stock of that quarter ([Motley Fool, Nov 2024](https://www.fool.com/investing/2024/11/15/down-80-from-its-all-time-high-is-super-micro-comp); [CNBC, Dec 2018](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/21/nvidia-is-the-worst-performing-sp-500-stock-this-quarter.html)). The academic prior is that after a 100% two-year industry run-up, slightly more than half of industries crash within two years, and volatility, turnover and share issuance raise the odds ([Greenwood, Shleifer and You, NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23191)). The honest working prior for a supplier stock already up 3x in this cycle is therefore better than even odds of a 40%-plus drawdown within two years of the top, and a 70 to 90% drawdown as the modal outcome if the capex cycle actually turns.

The 2000 to 2001 sequence also tells you what a turn looks like before it shows up in a supplier's own numbers. The broad index and the customers' stocks topped first (Nasdaq March 10, 2000; Cisco March 27), the component makers topped six to seven months later (Corning September 1; Juniper mid-October), supplier inventories tripled during the last two quarters of growth (Cisco's went from $470M to $1.7B during 2000), second-tier vendors cut forecasts months before the leader, the largest customer warned (Nortel, February 2001), the supplier's own guide broke (Corning cut its 2001 photonics growth outlook from 50% to 20 to 25%), and revenue then fell 30 to 45% within two to four quarters ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/266552/it-organization-what-went-wrong-at-cisco-in-2001.html); [Telecompaper](https://www.telecompaper.com/news/corning-cuts-2001-outlook--246463); [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2001/08/03/record-write-off-at-jds-uniphase/)). The stocks peaked four to ten months before revenue did. That sequence is the template for the leading-indicator section at the end of this report.

## September raised the cost of capital without denting the order book

The three weeks since the September 8 memo delivered the first genuine test of the AI trade's financing, and the order book passed while the discount rate failed. On September 16 the Federal Open Market Committee voted 12 to 0 to raise the fed funds target range 25 basis points to **3.75 to 4.00%**, the first increase since 2023, citing inflation "brought on by spiraling oil prices and other factors," with projections pointing to one more hike in 2026; the Dow fell more than 600 points that day, the 10-year yield topped 5% again and later in the month reached **5.13%, the highest since 2007**, with Brent and WTI above $100 per barrel ([CNBC, Sept 16](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html); [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Yahoo Finance live blog](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-wednesday-september-16-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-fed-meeting-decision-080356525.html); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/energy/treasury-yields-oil-stocks-rcna599398)). One secondary account has the Fed chair attributing part of the 10-year's climb to hyperscaler borrowing, which, if accurate, is a remarkable admission that AI capex is now large enough to move the risk-free rate ([Yahoo Finance / Barchart](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/nvidia-openai-oracles-745b-financing-135702453.html)). For a retail reader, the practical meaning is this: every dollar of 2027 capex now has to clear a higher hurdle, and the Meta/BlackRock El Paso data-center project bond, priced above 7% in late July and about 40 basis points wider than Meta's earlier Hyperion deal, shows the market already charging more for it ([MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/metas-125-billion-data-center-bond-signals-rising-capital-costs-across-ai-infrastructure); [Capacity](https://capacityglobal.com/news/metas-12bn-el-paso-bond-exposes-ai-debt-repricing/)).

Two days before the hike, the market showed how crowded the position is. On **September 14**, after Anthropic's Dario Amodei published an essay calling for slower frontier-model development and Sam Altman and Elon Musk said they agreed, the PHLX Semiconductor index fell **5.9% in one session**, trimming its 2026 gain to 57%; Nvidia fell about 3%, Broadcom 4.8%, Micron 6% to $916, SanDisk 6%, SK hynix 7%, and the Roundhill Memory ETF 7% against a 2% drop in the Nasdaq-100 ([CNN Business](https://us.cnn.com/2026/09/14/business/ai-stocks-slide-slowdown-development-amodei-altman-intl); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/14/memory-stocks-lead-ai-selloff-as-anthropic-and-openai-chiefs-urge-slower-development-micron-and-sandisk-sink-6-sk-hynix-drops-7/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/broadcom-ceo-on-anthropics-slowdown-push-ai-revenue-targets-havent-changed.html)). The logic cited that day is the one that matters for every supplier in this report: if progress slows, hyperscalers "can simply stop adding capacity and harvest the returns from what they already built," which hurts picks-and-shovels names far more than the hyperscalers themselves, and indeed Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet rose while Nvidia fell ([Fortune, Sept 14](https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slowdown-stocks-nvidia-meta/)). A non-financial catalyst producing a 6% one-day index decline is a template for tail risk, not a one-off. Broadcom's Hock Tan responded that the $115B fiscal-2027 and $230B fiscal-2028 AI revenue targets "haven't changed," and the week of September 26 ended with the Nasdaq at a record 27,122 and semiconductors the only group to outperform, led by AMD (+9.9%, to a $1T market cap) and Nvidia (+2.3%) ([STL.News, Sept 26](https://www.stl.news/u-s-stocks-end-higher-after-volatile-week-sept-26/); [SM Trader](https://smtraderca.substack.com/p/weekly-market-review-9252026)). A hawkish Fed and a record Nasdaq in the same month means investors treat AI earnings momentum as more certain than the rate path, which is exactly the configuration that historically precedes the crowded-trade unwind rather than prevents it.

Oracle's September 10 report is the clearest single data point on the state of the demand side. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue rose 30% to $19.3B, cloud revenue 62% to $11.6B, and remaining performance obligations rose $209B year over year to **$664B**, about $33B above consensus, after more than $30B of new AI cloud contracts in the quarter; fiscal-2027 guidance was raised to at least $90B of revenue ([Oracle](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/q1fy27-earnings-release-2026-09-10/); [CNBC, Sept 10](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/10/oracle-orcl-q1-earnings-report-2027.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/oracle-orcl-reports-664-billion-002207397.html)). But capex was **$28.5B in a single quarter**, up from $8.5B a year earlier and larger than total revenue, free cash flow was **negative $5.4B**, and the company carries about $125B of debt; the stock jumped 7 to 8.5% and then gave the gain back on cash-burn worries ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/oracle-orcl-earnings-cloud-62-percent-rpo-664-billion/); [FX Leaders, Sept 9](https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/09/09/oracle-stock-orcl-faces-major-earnings-test-with-heavy-debt-negative-free-cash-flow/); [AllWeatherFinance](https://allweatherfinance.com/oracle-shares-jumped-8-5-before-falling-back-strong-ai-demand-fails-to-dispel-concerns-about-massive-cash-burn-and-profits/)). Read together, the backlog says demand for Nvidia, AMD and memory is intact and accelerating in absolute terms, while the market is now discounting that backlog for the cash it takes to build it. Sell-side 2027 estimates moved up during the window: Goldman's September 25 note put 2027 AI infrastructure spending by the five largest US hyperscalers at **$1.2T, up more than half**, above a roughly $1.1T consensus and above Goldman's own earlier $1.01T ([Bloomberg, Sept 25](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-25/goldman-sees-hyperscaler-ai-capex-rising-50-to-1-2-trillion); [Goldman Sachs Insights](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/tracking-trillions-the-assumptions-shaping-scale-of-the-ai-build-out)). No first-party September statement from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon or Meta on 2027 capex, GPU depreciation lives or return on investment was found; the next hard read is the late-October earnings round.

Memory pricing, the current binding constraint, kept tightening. TrendForce's September 24 forecast raised fourth-quarter DRAM contract prices, with **server DRAM up 13 to 18%** quarter over quarter and PC DRAM up 3 to 8% (from 0 to 5%), citing cloud providers adding orders for agentic AI and tight supply ([TrendForce report page](https://www.trendforce.com/research/download/RP260924PL); [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/trendforce-raises-q3-q4-dram-contract-price-forecasts-benefits-micron)). Samsung's memory chief expects "significant shortages" through at least 2027, SK hynix's CEO called 2027 potentially "the most difficult year in the history of the memory industry from a supply perspective," HBM4 production-ready capacity is fully booked, and Samsung and SK hynix inventories reportedly fell below 10 days ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/samsung-and-sk-hynix-warn-ai-driven-memory-shortages-could-last-until-2027-and-beyond-as-hbm-demand-explodes-customers-already-reserving-supply-years-ahead-while-the-wider-dram-market-begins-to-tighten); [TechTimes, Sept 7](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326837/20260907/memory-runs-dry-samsung-sk-hynix-drop-below-10-day-supply-hbm4-devours-capacity.htm)). **Micron has not reported**: its fiscal Q4 2026 call is Wednesday, September 30 after the close, against a guide of $50B plus or minus $1B of revenue at roughly 86% gross margin and $31 plus or minus $1 of EPS, consensus around $50.5 to 51.2B and $31 to 31.6, and a stock that fell to $916 on September 14 and rebounded to about $1,093 into the print while Michael Burry disclosed an increased short position ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/26/3351673/14450/en/micron-technology-to-report-fiscal-fourth-quarter-results-on-september-30-2026.html); [AlphaStreet](https://news.alphastreet.com/micron-technology-mu-q4-2026-preview-eps-est-31-56-reports-september-30/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/micron-technology-shares-edge-lower-125050970.html)). Nothing in this report assumes a Micron result. The swing factor on September 30 is the fiscal Q1 2027 guide and HBM4 commentary, because Susquehanna has warned that Nvidia's Rubin mass production may slip to April 2027, pushing HBM4's substantive revenue contribution into 2027 ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/7e819768-999a-4d8c-8b53-9642aa548906)).

Nvidia itself was flat through all of this. It traded at $222.06 with a $5.53T market cap on September 23 ahead of the Trump-Xi summit, which ended September 25 without a chip deal (the US Trade Representative said semiconductors "weren't front and center"), and its guidance already assumes zero China data-center revenue, so the non-event cost nothing ([Yahoo Finance, Sept 23](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/nvidia-holds-flat-trump-xi-154237554.html); [MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/chip-stocks-slide-after-u-s-china-summit-ends-without-major-tech-deals-update-ce7f5bd3d989f721)). The one corporate event was the $12.93B Hugging Face acquisition, definitive as of September 8 and expected to close in the first half of 2027, which adds developer-platform lock-in but is immaterial to revenue ([NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/); [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000078/nvda-20260902.htm)). More telling for the cycle: Nvidia had not set a record high in four months as of September 20, its all-time high being May 14, and in 2000 the leader stalled months before the suppliers topped ([Motley Fool, Sept 20](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/20/nvidia-stock-hasn-t-set-a-record-in-4-months-history-says-these-stalls-have-ended-2-ways/)).

### Why the obvious names fail the test

Each of the household names fails on at least one of the five signatures, usually the fifth. Nvidia at $5.5T cannot 10x without becoming a $55T company. Micron has perfect fundamentals (revenue +346%, an 85% gross margin, 16 strategic customer agreements) but trades at about 6 to 7x fiscal-2027 consensus EPS of roughly $165, and a single-digit forward P/E on a memory company is the market pricing a cycle peak, not missing one; the CEO has sold more than $140M of stock since May, Burry is short, and the fourth-quarter number arrives on September 30 ([ValueInvesting.io](https://valueinvesting.io/MU/metric/forward-pe); [Uncover Alpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/every-memory-cycle-ends-the-same); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/micron-mu-stock-drop-sanjay-mehrotra-sale)). ## Credo keeps first place after a bear case that narrows the moat but does not break it

Credo sells the high-speed plumbing inside AI racks: active electrical cables (copper cables with a retimer chip inside that carry 800G and 1.6T signals a few meters, replacing optical links inside and between racks), optical DSPs, silicon-photonics chips and its "Zero-Flap" optical transceivers, all built on in-house SerDes IP. The reported numbers are the reason it topped the September 8 screen and still do. Fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended about August 1, reported September 1) revenue was **$479.0M, up 114.7% year over year and 9.6% sequentially**, the seventh straight triple-digit quarter; non-GAAP gross margin was 68.0% (GAAP 64.5%), non-GAAP operating margin 48.2%, non-GAAP EPS $1.20 against $1.12 expected; cash and short-term investments were $764.3M ([Credo 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162828026059795/credoq12027ex-991.htm); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/credo-reports-q1-fiscal-2027-revenue-of-479-million-up-115-year-over-year/)). Guidance was for fiscal Q2 revenue of $525 to 535M, fiscal-2027 growth "above 85%" with "an inflection in the second half," margins "broadly consistent with fiscal 2026," and optical revenue above $600M in fiscal 2027 with Zero-Flap optics, silicon-photonic PICs and optical DSPs each above $100M ([Credo Q1 FY27 call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/09/08/credo-crdo-q1-2027-earnings-call-transcript/)). A 48% operating margin on under $2B of annual revenue is a Nvidia-like profile; nobody earns that on cables unless the intellectual property is the product.

### What changed between September 8 and September 28

The stock did most of the changing. It fell about 19 to 20% on September 2, the day after the print, as investors focused on the GAAP gross-margin drop from 68.2% to 64.5%, a 32.5% rise in GAAP operating expenses, and top-three customer concentration of about 74%; it bottomed near $150 in mid-September, then rose nine straight sessions for a cumulative 41% to close at **$210.97 on September 25** ([Motley Fool, Sept 2](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/02/why-credo-technology-stock-plunged-20-today/); [Insider Monkey](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/credo-crdo-reports-115-revenue-growth-but-lower-gaap-gross-margin-can-ai-connectivity-scale-without-further-margin-compression-1826282/); [Trefis, Sept 26](https://www.trefis.com/stock/crdo/articles/616737/a-9-day-winning-streak-has-credo-technology-stock-up-41/2026-09-26)). The rally's drivers were the ECOC 2026 showcase of 1.6T optical connectivity in Málaga (shares +6% that morning), Rosenblatt raising its target to $235 while keeping a Neutral rating, a new Buy rating, a widely circulated piece calling Credo "one of the most mispriced setups in AI infrastructure" at 43% below its 52-week high, and a broad semiconductor rebound ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/why-is-credo-technology-stock-rallying-today-93CH-4909330); [Rosenblatt via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/rosenblatt-raises-credo-tech-stock-price-target-on-earnings-outlook-93CH-4886038)). Mizuho, by contrast, kept its Outperform but **cut** its target to $245 from $290 "on valuation," a move that is easy to misread as bullish ([Investing.com NG](https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/credo-tech-stock-price-target-lowered-to-245-by-mizuho-on-valuation-93CH-2702248)). No new 10% customer, no named Zero-Flap design win and no explicit 1.6T AEC attach on Nvidia's Rubin racks was disclosed in the window. The underlying research notes inferred a September 25 price of "$230 to 245" from the analyst-target math; that inference is wrong and the verified close of $210.97 is used throughout. At that price the stock sits 11% below Rosenblatt's Neutral-rated $235, 16% below Mizuho's $245, and 33% below the $281 consensus average that stood on September 8 ([StockAnalysis forecast](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crdo/)).

The valuation consequence is arithmetic. On September 7 the forward P/E was 22.5x (StockAnalysis) to 26.8x (GuruFocus) at $170.57 ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/forward-pe-ratio/CRDO)). A September 25 forward P/E was not retrievable. The price is 23.7% higher, so unless consensus estimates rose in the interval (the Mizuho cut suggests they did not rise much), the multiple is proportionally higher, roughly the high-20s to low-30s; that is a derived range, not a sourced figure. A PEG (forward P/E divided by growth rate) near 0.3 on September 8 is now nearer 0.35 to 0.4 on the same arithmetic: still far below 1, still the "market does not believe the guide" configuration, but a smaller discount than three weeks ago. The F score therefore falls from 5 to 4, and the market-cap headroom score stays at 5 because $37 to 39B remains well under $50B.

### The bull case, restated with the new evidence

The bull case rests on six legs. First, margin structure says pricing power: a 68% non-GAAP gross margin and 48% operating margin at this scale compare with Nvidia's roughly 54% operating margin at $61B of revenue in fiscal 2024. Second, growth is guided, not hoped: fiscal 2026 revenue was $1.34B (+206%), so "above 85%" implies at least about $2.5B for fiscal 2027, and the company has beaten its own guide for seven quarters ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crdo/)). Third, the multiple is below the growth rate even after the rally. Fourth, headroom exists: at $37 to 39B a 10x is $370 to 390B, which needs roughly $12 to 15B of revenue at Credo's margins, against management's own "$10B-plus" addressable-market framing, Dell'Oro's roughly $1T of cumulative AI back-end switch spend over 2026 to 2030 and LightCounting's path to $100B of AI-cluster optics by 2030 ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/06/60023965/credo-wires-agentic-ai-with-10-billion-plus-market-in-sight); [Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/news/ai-back-end-switch-sales-to-approach-1-trillion-over-the-next-five-years/); [LightCounting](https://www.lightcounting.com/newsletter/en/march-2026-ethernet-optics-382)). Fifth, the customer base is widening: Amazon was 67% of fiscal-2025 revenue, the fiscal-2026 10-K shows two customers above 10%, and the top three were 74% in the latest quarter versus 88% cited for fiscal Q3 2026 in an earlier note, although the two figures may use different definitions ([StockTitan 10-K summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CRDO/10-k-credo-technology-group-holding-ltd-files-annual-report-77dc27617f91.html); [Al Capital Advisory](https://alcapitaladvisory.com/research/equities/crdo.html)). Sixth, the demand proxy is loud: Nvidia's data-center networking revenue grew 138% year over year in its latest quarter, Spectrum-X 2.6x, and every rack Nvidia and its custom-ASIC rivals ship needs more copper and optical links per accelerator, not fewer ([SEC 8-K Q2 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm)). September added one genuinely new bull datapoint: Nvidia's own architecture statements at GTC 2026 keep copper in-rack for Vera Rubin and Vera Rubin Ultra, with co-packaged optics out-of-rack, and reserve "native optical NVLink" for Feynman in 2028; that gives Credo's core AEC business a two-generation runway before the optical inflection that the "copper company in an optical world" bears fear ([The Register, Apr 5 2026](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2026/04/05/nvidia-embraces-optical-scale-up-as-copper-reaches-limits/5225238); [Radiant](https://radiant.co/blog/nvidia-vera-rubin-ultra-ushers-in-the-cpo-era)).

### The bear case, built deliberately

This report set out to break the Credo thesis, and four mechanisms survived scrutiny. The first is competitive entry into AEC silicon. Marvell launched its "Golden Cable" initiative in December 2025 to accelerate hyperscaler AEC adoption with validated reference designs for cable partners, its 1.6T AEC DSPs have been generally available since April 2025 with ecosystem demonstrations from 3M, Amphenol, Broadex, Luxshare and TE Connectivity, and at OFC 2026 Luxshare and Marvell showed a hybrid AEC/ACC Y-cable; Marvell also pushes cheaper "active copper cables" and a 9-meter 800G AEC for scale-up ([Marvell newsroom](https://www.marvell.com/company/newsroom/marvell-launches-strategic-initiative-to-accelerate-aec-ecosystem-and-hyperscaler-adoption.html); [Marvell blog](https://www.marvell.com/blogs/hybrid-aec-acc-cables-for-optimizing-infrastructure.html); [Semi Fundamental](https://semifundamental.substack.com/p/active-electrical-cable-aec-fundamentals)). Broadcom lists a BCM87851 retimer PHY optimized for active copper cables, Astera sells its own AEC module for PCIe and CXL, and Molex and Broadex market AEC lines ([Broadcom product page](https://www.broadcom.com/products/ethernet-connectivity/phy-and-poe/optical/bcm87851); [Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/technologies/embedded/article/21282733/electronic-design-astera-labs-rolls-out-active-electrical-cable-module-for-cxl-and-pcie)). The second is that Credo's own patent strategy has opened the door: after filing an ITC complaint against Amphenol, Molex, TE Connectivity and Volex, Credo reached confidential cross-licensing settlements by March 2026 with at least TE and Molex (one summary says all four), which "validate Credo's IP position and likely generate licensing revenue, but also legitimize competitors entering the AEC market with licensed technology" ([Credo press release](https://credosemi.com/news/credo-files-aec-patent-infringement-complaint-against-amphenol-molex-teconnectivity-and-volex-with-united-states-international-trade-commission/); [Semi Fundamental](https://semifundamental.substack.com/p/active-electrical-cable-aec-fundamentals)). Whether Amphenol and Volex actually settled is unresolved, and it matters, because Amphenol (27.3% adjusted operating margin, book-to-bill 1.23, IT datacom up 63% organically) owns the cable plant that Marvell's silicon needs ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260729372358/en/Amphenol-Reports-Record-Second-Quarter-2026-Results)). The third is margin. GAAP gross margin fell 370 basis points in one quarter, management guides fiscal-2027 margins only "broadly consistent" with fiscal 2026, and industry analysis expects AEC gross margins to settle at 62 to 65% as competition arrives; that gives Credo's 68% non-GAAP figure three to six points of downside before the optical mix shift, which carries lower margins by construction. The fourth is concentration and the shape of the guide. With top-three customers at 74 to 88% of revenue, a single hyperscaler dual-sourcing 30% of its AEC volume would remove roughly 10 to 25% of revenue; and because fiscal-2027 growth "above 85%" on $1.34B implies at least about $2.5B, with roughly $1.0B booked in the first two quarters, the second half must deliver about $1.4 to 1.5B, or roughly $700M per quarter versus $479M in the first quarter, depending on a handful of programs (Rubin-era 1.6T AEC attach, Zero-Flap optics, optical DSPs) any one of which slipping a quarter breaks the guide ([Top1Markets](https://www.top1markets.com/insights/stocks/credo-crdo-stock-earnings-guidance-math-2026)).

What the bear case did not find is as important as what it did. No source surfaced an announced hyperscaler AEC loss, a confirmed Amazon dual-sourcing decision, a Marvell earnings-call quantification of AEC revenue or a named hyperscaler AEC win, or any published short-seller report in 2026. Short interest was 6.67M shares, about 3.7% of float, at the August 31 settlement date, so the bears are not positioned ([Equibles](https://equibles.com/stocks/crdo/short-interest)). Insider selling is real but routine: the CEO sold 54,984 shares in June under a 10b5-1 plan and gifted 75,000 to trusts in July, and the COO sold 55,998 shares for $12.6M around July 16; no September Form 4s were checked ([StockTitan Form 4](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CRDO/form-4-credo-technology-group-holding-ltd-insider-trading-activity-c03ecffaf332.html); [Motley Fool filings](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/07/16/credo-technology-coo-sells-55998-shares-for-126-million/)). The September 2 drop reads as expectations resetting after a run into the print, not as a thesis break. The most credible near-term bear catalyst is therefore not Nvidia's CPO roadmap (in-rack copper survives through Rubin Ultra) but share and price erosion inside Credo's existing hyperscaler sockets as Marvell-DSP cables from Amphenol, Luxshare and TE qualify during the very second-half ramp the guide depends on. That is the mechanism by which this stock could halve within six months, and it is the reason the A score drops from 4 to 3.

### Does the September 8 ranking survive?

Yes, with a caveat that should be stated plainly. On the scorecard Credo falls from 27 to 24, and Lumentum holds at 24, so the two are tied; Credo keeps first place on two tiebreakers. Its market cap is roughly half Lumentum's last-sourced $79B, so the arithmetic path to a 5 to 10x is shorter, and its forward multiple remains below its growth rate while Lumentum's, at roughly 60x on the annualized fiscal Q1 EPS guide, does not. Lumentum, however, is the better answer to the literal question of "dominant supplier of a binding constraint," and the September evidence strengthened that claim (see the runners-up section). A reader who weights bottleneck ownership above valuation would reasonably rank Lumentum first; this report keeps Credo first because the Nvidia signature that mattered most in 2023 was the gap between guided earnings and the multiple, and Credo still has the wider gap. The conviction is lower than on September 8: the setup was "cheap after a 20% drop on a beat" then, and it is "fair after a 41% rally on no news" now.

## Lumentum and Astera hold as runners-up; SiTime, Onto and BESI enter the watch list

**Lumentum (LITE)** is the "memory of optics" and the September evidence made that description more precise. The AI cluster moves from 800G to 1.6T links in 2026 and 2027; a 1.6T link needs 200G-per-lane EML lasers built on indium-phosphide capacity that takes years to add; Lumentum holds an estimated 50 to 60% of that laser and is described as the only supplier shipping 200G-per-lane EMLs at volume ([Top1Markets](https://www.top1markets.com/news/lumentum-stock-china-optical-ban-fm26); [TechTimes, May 27](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317281/20260527/ai-data-center-optical-component-shortage-nvidias-4b-laser-lockup-pushes-rivals-past-2027.htm)). TrendForce sizes the 2026 AI optical transceiver market at $26B, expects 1.6T shipments of only about 15M units against a gap of roughly 10M units, and names EML laser shortages as the constraint; LightCounting has demand exceeding supply by about 30%; Nvidia has reportedly locked up EML supply with about $4B of investment in Lumentum and Coherent, pushing rivals past 2027 ([TrendForce, Apr 20](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260420-13017.html); [LightCounting](https://www.lightcounting.com/newsletter/en/april-2026-market-forecast-379)). That is the same shape as HBM in 2025: a component nobody thought about, a two-year supply lag, and a customer who cannot ship without it. The financials match: fiscal Q4 2026 revenue was $1.01B (+109%), the first $1B quarter, at a 50.4% non-GAAP gross margin and 36.6% operating margin, with fiscal Q1 2027 guided to $1.225 to 1.275B at a 39.5 to 40.5% operating margin (more than 2,100 basis points of year-over-year expansion) and EPS of $4.05 to 4.35; 1.6T modules enter volume production this quarter, CPO products ship in volume to Nvidia in the second half, and orders are reportedly booked through 2028 ([Lumentum 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001633978/000162828026055726/lite_ex991xq4fy26.htm); [Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605653826)). Operating margin from roughly 15% to 40% in a year is the fastest margin expansion of any company in this study, faster than Nvidia's own 2023.

Two things hold Lumentum in second. Size and multiple: the last sourced market cap was about $79B on September 4 at about $1,053, roughly 60x the annualized fiscal Q1 EPS guide, and the September 25 price was not retrievable; optics names "fell sharply" through early and mid-September and then bounced on short covering (Coherent +6%, Lumentum +5% on September 16; Lumentum +3%, Coherent +5% on September 18), so the cap may be lower than $79B, but that cannot be confirmed ([StockAnalysis LITE](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lite/market-cap/); [24/7 Wall St, Sept 16](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/16/optics-stocks-rally-as-oversold-bounce-takes-hold-coherent-jumps-6-lumentum-climbs-5-applied-optoelectronics-advances-3/)). A 5x from $79B is $400B, which needs $10B-plus of revenue. And the FCC rule is two-edged: the agency is drafting a ban on imports of new models of Chinese optical transceivers, targeted for finalization before the end of 2026, which lifts Western share in principle; but one estimate says a ban would cut 60% of AI data-center transceiver supply, Western vendors "don't yet have sufficient capacity to fill the gap," and both Lumentum and Coherent depend on indium phosphide subject to Chinese export constraints, so the same policy that hands Lumentum share could cap what it can ship ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4205228/with-fcc-ban-on-new-chinese-made-optical-transceivers-for-dcs-likely-it-may-be-time-to-stock-up.html); [TechTimes, Aug 5](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323104/20260805/fcc-transceiver-ban-would-cut-60-ai-data-center-supply-western-replacements-need-chinese-indium.htm)). The halving risk is a hyperscaler or Nvidia move to CPO with in-house or Broadcom-sourced lasers, a China InP restriction, or the 2028 optics glut that the industry produced in 2001. Coherent is the safer way to own the same shortage (Nvidia's $2B equity partner, a $3B quarter targeted by end of fiscal 2027) but its 40.2% gross margin and a shrinking Industrial segment (-15.8%) make it the compounder, not the blowup ([Coherent IR](https://ir.coherent.com/news-releases/news-release-details/coherent-corp-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2026)).

**Astera Labs (ALAB)** remains the "paradigm" pick: if custom accelerators become half the market, every non-Nvidia rack needs a merchant scale-up fabric, and Astera's 320-lane Scorpio X-Series switch is the merchant answer, positioned across PCIe, CXL, Ethernet, UALink and now NVLink Fusion for "hybrid racks" ([Astera press release](https://www.asteralabs.com/news/astera-labs-extends-leadership-in-open-ai-scale-up-networking-with-new-320-lane-scorpio-x-series-smart-fabric-switch/); [Astera custom solutions](https://www.asteralabs.com/news/astera-labs-expands-connectivity-portfolio-with-custom-solutions/)). Q2 revenue was $392.4M (+104%, +27% sequentially) at a 73.7% gross margin, the Q3 guide of $540 to 560M is a 40% sequential jump on a Scorpio X ramp at an unnamed customer, five customers each exceed 10% of revenue, and Jefferies raised its target to $450 from $270 ([Astera IR](https://ir.asteralabs.com/news-releases/news-release-details/astera-labs-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results); [Astera 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001736297/000173629726000035/alab-20260630.htm); [Quiver Quant](https://www.quiverquant.com/news/Astera+Labs+Jumps+as+Investors+Revisit+AI+Growth+Story+Ahead+of+September+Conference)). The stock was about $364.62 "in late September" (exact date not given), which on roughly 185M shares implies a cap in the mid-$60Bs, a derived figure; the September 8 sources conflicted between $54B and $72B ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/astera-labs-inc-alab-exceeds-211501474.html)). It ranks third for the same reason as before: at roughly 120x forward earnings the market already believes the paradigm, whereas Nvidia at $1T in mid-2023 was about 50x. September added a structural wrinkle in Astera's own roadmap (pluggable AEC and near-packaged optics in 2027, CPO switches in 2028), which mirrors Nvidia's copper-then-optical timeline and makes Astera an Nvidia-adjacent competitor to Credo in PCIe and UALink copper links, and no September design win was disclosed ([Vik's Newsletter](https://www.viksnewsletter.com/p/astera-labs-from-the-tray-to-the-rack)). What would move it to first is unchanged: a second consecutive quarter of 30%-plus sequential growth with operating margin above 45%, and a disclosed design win on a Broadcom-built XPU rack for OpenAI or Anthropic.

Three names enter the watch list, none yet scorable. **SiTime (SITM)** makes MEMS timing chips (the tiny oscillators that keep every chip in a server synchronized) and closed the Renesas timing business on July 1, 2026, adding more than 550 clocking products, about 10,000 customers, and a targeted $300M of revenue at a 70% gross margin in the first twelve months, with about 75% of the acquired revenue in AI, data center and communications ([SiTime close PR](https://www.sitime.com/company/newsroom/press-release/sitime-completes-acquisition-renesas-timing-business); [SiTime 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001451809/000145180926000045/ex992-renesastimingbusines.htm)). It closed at $643 to 662 on September 25 (sources differ) on about 30M shares, a cap of roughly $19 to 20B, the smallest of any candidate discussed, after Morgan Stanley initiated at Overweight with a $730 target on September 17 and the stock joined the PHLX Semiconductor index on September 21; it is still about 30% below its $901.81 52-week high ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/nebenwerte/sitime-stock-extends-2026-rally-after-morgan-stanley-overweight-rating/70137006); [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/semiconductors/nasdaq-sitm/sitime/news/how-index-inclusion-at-sitime-stock-has-changed-its-investme); [Yahoo/Insider Monkey](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/sitime-corporation-sitm-positioned-heart-144549464.html)). The gaps are decisive for now: its latest-quarter revenue growth, Q3 guide, forward P/E and the size of the 2026 equity offering implied by a Form 424B5 were not retrieved, so the D and F scores cannot be assigned, and the 30% drawdown despite an upgrade and index inclusion suggests the market is discounting integration and dilution risk ([SEC 424B5](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001451809/000119312526233298/d132434d424b5.htm)). It is on the list because it is the one sub-$25B name with a plausible "dominant supplier" claim (timing) whose acquisition just doubled its exposure to the constraint layer; it is not a pick until the growth figure is known.

**Onto Innovation (ONTO)** and **Camtek (CAMT)** form a near-duopoly in inspection and metrology for advanced packaging and HBM, the equipment that checks the stacked chips before they ship. Onto's Q2 revenue was a record $343M (+35%), with advanced-packaging growth for 2026 raised to about 80%, a Q3 guide of about $390M that implies year-over-year growth above 50%, and a record backlog above $1.1B with 30 to 40% covering 2027; Camtek derives about 75% of revenue from advanced packaging, guides Q3 to $158 to 160M (+20% sequentially) and has booked more than $600M of orders year to date against a roughly $130M quarterly revenue run rate ([Onto IR](https://investors.ontoinnovation.com/news/news-details/2026/Onto-Innovation-Reports-2026-Second-Quarter-Results/default.aspx); [Camtek via PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/camtek-announces-results-for-the-second-quarter-of-2026-302847042.html)). Neither's market cap or forward P/E as of September 25 was retrievable, Onto's gross margin was not captured, and Morgan Stanley and Stifel trimmed Camtek targets in a "reset to sector multiples" ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/CAMT/forecast/)). **BESI** is the closest thing to a hybrid-bonding monopoly (Q2 orders +129%, revenue +68.7%, 21 hybrid-bonding customers, long-term revenue target raised to 1.7 to 2.2B euros), but the memory leg has slipped: HBM4 sticks with microbumps and SK hynix points to HBM5 for hybrid bonding, so the memory catalyst is a 2027 order event, and the shares are Euronext-listed with only an OTC ADR ([Investing.com on BESI](https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/besi-reports-strong-q2-earnings-on-aidriven-chip-packaging-demand-4807530); [Semiconductor Engineering](https://semiengineering.com/hbm4-sticks-with-microbumps-postponing-hybrid-bonding/)). These three are where the next constraint (substrates and packaging, where Counterpoint models a 20% ABF gap in 2027) can be owned through US or ADR listings, and they deserve a full screen once valuation data is available.

## Systemic risks and the indicators that would mark the turn

Every candidate in this report sells into the most discretionary part of a buildout that is now externally financed, and the September evidence sharpened four systemic risks. The first is funding cost, which is the single most important change since September 8. AI capex has gone from 33% of hyperscalers' operating cash flow in 2023 to an estimated 93% in 2026; incremental annual debt rose from 9% of capex in fiscal 2024 to 32% by mid-2026 with equity back in the mix; Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Oracle issued about $194B of bonds in 2026 through July 7, up 79% on all of 2025, with spreads widening ([Fortune, Sept 14](https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slowdown-stocks-nvidia-meta/); [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/hyperscalers-tap-external-financing-as-ai-capex-outruns-cash-flow); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/hyperscaler-debt-binge-pushes-yields-134825104.html)). With the risk-free rate above 5% and project debt above 7%, Jefferies' Chris Wood put it bluntly: his base case is "massive loss of capital destruction in the U.S." on roughly a trillion dollars of 2027 investment, with a shift to debt financing raising the risk of a funding break ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/09/61868964/ai-capex-boom-risks-massive-capital-destruction-in-the-us-warns-veteran-market-strategist-you-could-get-some-news-item-that-suddenly)). The counter-argument, that the buildout is financed largely from the operating cash flow of companies that were already profitable, remains intact, and Goldman raised rather than cut its 2027 number ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/the-ai-bubble)). Both can be true: demand is real and the cost of meeting it just rose. In Greenwood-Shleifer-You terms, issuance (Alphabet's $84.75B equity raise in June, Oracle's $20B at-the-market program, neocloud secured debt, the SpaceX, Cerebras and coming Anthropic listings) is one of the attributes that raises crash probability.

The second is circularity. Nvidia invests in OpenAI (up to $100B), OpenAI buys compute from Oracle ($300B) and Microsoft ($250B) and AWS ($138B), Oracle buys Nvidia chips; more than $800B of such arrangements exist, Nvidia's CFO has acknowledged that "some will call this circular financing," and Nvidia has also invested in Coherent, CoreWeave and Nebius while AMD pays OpenAI in warrants and Google backstops TeraWulf and Cipher ([Global Finance](https://gfmag.com/technology/the-circle-game/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/nvidia-openai-oracles-745b-financing-135702453.html)). Demand and financing are entangled in a way they were not in 2023, so a funding break at one node propagates. The third is the model-layer squeeze. The Silicon Data LLM Token Expenditure Index hit a record low of $0.97 per million tokens on September 1, more than 50% below early-summer peaks, while GPU rental prices are rising (Nebius announced 17 to 21% price increases for October 1, its second hike in three months); falling output prices and rising input prices squeeze the labs' gross margins, and the labs are the marginal buyers of everything in this report ([TechJack Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/llm-token-prices-record-low-below-1-per-million-tokens/); [24/7 Wall St, Sept 17](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/17/iren-climbs-6-nebius-jumps-10-as-gpu-rental-rates-head-higher-coreweave-slips/)). The fourth is capital rotation: Anthropic's public S-1 had not appeared on EDGAR as of September 26 despite reports it would land after Labor Day, and prediction markets now put a listing by December 31 at 74 to 78%; OpenAI has said no 2026 IPO. A roughly $1T-plus listing in the fourth quarter pulls capital out of public AI proxies ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-anthropic-s-1-that-was-expected-after-labor-day-still-hasnt-arrived/); [Prediction News](https://predictionnews.com/story/polymarket-traders-shift-anthropic-ipo-odds-toward-december-2026); [Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-stocks-sink-amodei-altman-musk-slowdown-091426)). Two further risks are unchanged: Nvidia's gravity (it is the number-one data-center Ethernet vendor, licenses NVLink, bought Groq's team and is buying Hugging Face, so any merchant rack-component supplier competes with its largest customer's ambitions), and China (no summit deal; Chinese vendors hold about 60% of 800G optics volume, so an FCC ban helps Lumentum and a thaw hurts it) ([The Register, Aug 31](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/31/nvidia-is-building-an-ip-licensing-empire-on-the-back-of-nvlink/5293530); [Cignal AI](https://cignal.ai/2026/08/fcc-ban-on-new-chinese-optical-modules/)).

Mapping the 2000 to 2001 sequence onto September 2026 gives a status check on the cycle. Stage one, the leader stalls, is present: Nvidia had gone four months without a record as of September 20 and the SOX fell 6% in a day while hyperscalers rose. Stage two, a supplier inventory build, is not visible; the sourced signals point the other way, with Nvidia calling itself supply-constrained, its purchase commitments up from $119B to $279B in a quarter, and memory 20% under-supplied by SK hynix's own account ([SEC 8-K Q2 FY27](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000073/q2fy27pr.htm); [TrendForce, Aug 4](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260804-13166.html)). Stage three, second-tier vendors cutting forecasts, is partial: Marvell fell 7% on its guide, Broadcom's Q4 guide missed, Ciena fell 10% on an in-line guide, but each was a mix or expectations issue, not a demand cut. Stage four, the largest customer warning, is absent: every company in the latest capex round raised or held guidance and none cut, with seven builders at $657B of trailing-four-quarter capex ([Supercycle](https://supercyclehq.com/blog/reports/hyperscaler-capex-september-2026)). Stages five through seven are absent. On the 2000 template, the cycle sits at the "stocks top before fundamentals" stage, roughly where Cisco was in spring and summer 2000, if it is topping at all, and the financing indicators are the most 2000-like part of the picture.

## Conclusion

The rerun changes the confidence more than the answer. On September 8 Credo was a high-conviction pick because the market had just marked a 115%-growth, 48%-operating-margin company down 20% on a beat to roughly 25x forward earnings; three weeks later the same company is 24% more expensive on no new information, its patent settlements have licensed the cable makers into its category, and Marvell's silicon has a route to its sockets through Amphenol, Luxshare and TE. None of that breaks the thesis, and the bears are not even positioned (3.7% short interest), but it converts "cheap" into "fair" and lowers the A and F scores enough that Lumentum now ties it on the scorecard. The useful reframing for a retail reader is that the two names answer two different halves of the question: Lumentum is the cleanest living example of "dominant supplier of a binding constraint" (200G EML lasers, a 10M-unit 1.6T gap, orders through 2028, margin expansion faster than Nvidia's own), while Credo is the cleanest example of the valuation configuration that made Nvidia's 2023 possible (guided earnings running far ahead of the multiple). Credo keeps first place because that configuration is the rarer and more time-limited of the two, and because a $38B cap leaves arithmetic room that $79B does not. If the second-half ramp prints at roughly $700M a quarter and the 10-Q shows a fourth hyperscaler above 10%, the conviction returns; if Marvell names an AEC win at a top-three Credo customer, the ranking flips to Lumentum the same day.

The larger implication is about the cycle rather than the stock. September delivered the first month in which the AI trade's demand side strengthened (RPO $664B, 2027 capex estimates to $1.2T, DRAM prices up again) while its financing side weakened (a Fed hike, a 5.13% ten-year, 7% project bonds, capex at 93% of operating cash flow) and its leader stalled for a fourth month. That is the exact configuration of spring 2000, when the suppliers had six to seven more months of gains ahead of them and then lost 90 to 99%. Every name in this report carries a beta near 3 and would fall 40 to 60% in a risk-off tape without any change to its fundamentals, and the base rate says the odds of a 40%-plus drawdown within two years of the top are better than even. The right posture for anyone acting on this research is to size for that outcome, to treat the late-October hyperscaler capex guides as the decision point rather than the September rally, and to remember that the historical 10x winners were bought when their growth was 7%, not 115%.
