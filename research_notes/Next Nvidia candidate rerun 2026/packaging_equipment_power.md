## Key Question 1: Which component is the next bottleneck after memory in 2027, and who are the dominant suppliers?

### Takeaway
As of late Sept 2026, memory (DRAM/HBM) is the acknowledged binding constraint with 2027 capacity reportedly fully sold. The consensus "next" constraints for 2027 are (a) high-end ABF/T-glass substrates, where Counterpoint models a ~10% gap in H2'26 widening to ~20% in 2027 with Nittobo the dominant T-glass supplier; (b) optical components (200G-per-lane EMLs, InP), where Lumentum is described as the only volume supplier and the 1.6T transceiver gap is ~10M units; and (c) grid/power equipment (transformers at ~128-144-week lead times, gas turbines booked through 2030). CoWoS is easing (gap narrowing from ~20% to ~10% by end-2026 per TrendForce-relayed reports) and is no longer the primary constraint.

### Inferences
- Ranking of 2027 constraints by severity of quantified gap: (1) memory/HBM (100% of 2027 allocated; 60-70% fill rates), (2) ABF/T-glass substrates (Counterpoint ~20% gap in 2027; sole-source T-glass at Nittobo), (3) EML/InP optics (~10M-unit 1.6T gap; single volume supplier), (4) grid equipment (2.5-3 year transformer lead times; turbines booked through 2030), (5) CoWoS (gap shrinking to ~10%).
- Dominant suppliers of the 2027 constraints are mostly NOT US-listed: Nittobo (T-glass, Japan), Ibiden/Unimicron/AT&S/Samsung Electro-Mechanics (ABF), SK hynix/Samsung (HBM). US-listed exposure to the constraint layer is largely via equipment/inspection (CAMT, ONTO, NVMI, BESI ADR, TER, FORM, COHU) and optics (Lumentum, Coherent — outside this brief's list) and power (GEV, ETN, VRT, POWL, BE).
- Hybrid bonding is a 2027-28 memory story, not 2026; BESI's 2026 revenue inflection is logic/CPO-driven.
