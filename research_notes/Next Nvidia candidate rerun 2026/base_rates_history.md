## Key Question 1: What did the 10x+ winners have in common at the START of their runs (size, growth, margin, multiple, customer concentration, supply constraint)?

### Takeaway
Across four cycles the sourced starting profile is remarkably consistent: a small-to-mid cap (single-digit billions to low tens of billions; Nvidia's 2022 start at ~$300B is the outlier), revenue growth that was *not yet* spectacular at the start (Nvidia FY16 +7%, AMD 2016 +7%, Vertiv 2023 +21%, SMCI FQ2'23 +54%) but that then went triple-digit within 2-4 quarters, gross margins that ranged from very high (Nvidia, Arista 66%, Credo 68%, Astera 74%) to very low (SMCI 18.7%, Celestica ~10%, AMD 23%), and, critically, a physical supply constraint (fiber/optics 2000, CoWoS/HBM 2023, memory 2025-26, EML lasers 2026). Starting multiples were rarely retrievable; where they were, the winners were cheap on *forward* numbers because consensus lagged guidance for many quarters.

### Inferences
- Size at start: every sourced 10x+ started under ~$20B (JDSU pre-1999, Juniper, First Solar, AMD ~$1-2B, Arista ~$3B at IPO, SMCI, Vertiv, Credo, Astera). Nvidia's 2022 start at ~$300-340B is the exception that proves the rule: it needed a $5T+ endpoint, i.e. the largest company in the world, to deliver 16-20x.
- Growth at start is a weak screen; acceleration is the screen. The two best-documented 10x+ winners (Nvidia 2016, AMD 2016) were growing 7% when their runs began. What they shared was an inflection within 2-4 quarters (Nvidia data center +133% by FY18; AMD +490% stock in 12 months on a 15% revenue quarter).
- Gross margin at start does not discriminate: the set includes 18.7% (SMCI), 23% (AMD), ~10% (Celestica), 33% (Bloom) alongside 66-74% (Arista, Credo, Astera, Nvidia). But *margin expansion during the run* does: Nvidia 57→75%, Vertiv op margin 7→22%, Lumentum +2,100 bp, Micron to 85%. Low-margin winners (SMCI, Celestica) are the ones whose runs ended fastest.
- Customer concentration is the norm, not a disqualifier, at the start (Arista ~40% from two customers; Credo 67% Amazon; Astera 5 customers ≥10%; Celestica 63% from three). It becomes the failure channel at the end (see KQ2/KQ3).
- The single most consistent signature at the start is that the company sells the *binding constraint* of the buildout (fiber/lasers 2000; GPUs 2023; HBM/DRAM 2025-26; EML lasers 2026) and consensus is behind guidance, so the forward multiple compresses as the price rises.

My recollections (AMD ~$1.5B at a ~$1.6 low in Feb 2016; Nvidia ~$15B in Jan 2016; SMCI ~$3-4B in late 2022) are unverified and should not be cited without a check.

## Key Question 2: What fraction of "picks and shovels" names up 3x+ in a capex boom subsequently fell 70%+ when the cycle turned, and how early did the turn show up in the data (orders, book-to-bill, customer capex guides, inventory)?

### Takeaway
No formal study of exactly this fraction was found. In the 2000-02 telecom bust, every supplier name for which a peak-to-trough figure was sourced fell 88-99.8% (Cisco -89%, JDSU -99.8%, Corning -99%, EMC -96.5%); the Nasdaq itself fell 78%. The academic industry-level base rate (Greenwood, Shleifer & You) is that after a 100% two-year run-up, slightly more than half of industries crash within two years. The turn showed up in supplier data (inventory build, other vendors' forecast cuts, customer warnings) roughly 3-6 months before the affected company's own guidance broke, but the stocks themselves peaked 4-10 months before revenue did.

### Inferences
- Fraction falling 70%+: in the 2000-02 sample with sourced data (Cisco, JDSU, Corning, EMC, plus Nasdaq -78%), 4 of 4 named suppliers fell more than 85%. Adding sourced later-cycle cases (First Solar ~-96%, SMCI -80%, Nvidia 2018 at least -31% calendar-year and worse peak-to-trough), 6 of 6 named supplier winners with data fell >70% at the cycle turn. This is a sample of names that were *selected because they were famous casualties*, so it overstates the true base rate; the Greenwood-Shleifer-You industry-level ~50%+ crash rate after a 100% run-up is the honest unconditional prior, and the supplier-specific conditional rate is plausibly higher (they are the highest-beta expression of the cycle).
- Lead time of the signal: Cisco's inventory tripled during calendar 2000 while the stock had already peaked in March 2000; other networking vendors cut forecasts "months" before Cisco's Feb 2001 break; Nortel warned in Feb 2001; JDSU's revenue peaked in the Sept 2000 quarter. So (a) stocks peaked 4-10 months before supplier revenue peaked, (b) inventory and lead-time data turned ~2 quarters before guidance, and (c) once guidance broke, revenue fell 30-45% within 2-3 quarters.
- Speed: from first guidance break to a >70% stock decline took ~6-8 months in 2001 (Corning: guidance cut early 2001, stock at single digits by late 2001) and ~6-8 months for SMCI in 2024. Full trough took 24-30 months in 2000-02.
- The 2018 Nvidia episode is the cleanest template for "how a supplier's numbers break": a channel-inventory overhang turns a $3.4B expected quarter into $2.2B in ten weeks, and the stock loses roughly half before fundamentals trough.

## Key Question 3: Optical components (JDSU, Corning, Finisar 1999-2002) and networking (Cisco, Juniper): what were the leading indicators of the 2001 bust and how fast did it happen?

### Takeaway
The optics bust was a demand front-loading unwind: carriers had laid fiber of which only a small fraction was lit, component makers had grown 70-400% on acquisitions and double-ordering, and when Nortel warned in February 2001 the whole chain repriced within one quarter. Revenue at JDSU fell 35% in three quarters, Finisar 45% in four, Corning cut its 2001 photonics growth guide from 50% to 20-25%, and stocks that had peaked in Sept-Oct 2000 were down 90%+ by late 2001 and 99% by Oct 2002.

## Key Question 5: What do academic or practitioner studies say about return distributions for suppliers into a capex boom?

### Takeaway
The sourced literature is thin but directionally unanimous: sharp industry run-ups more than double the odds of a subsequent crash (Greenwood-Shleifer-You), semiconductor suppliers see revenue fall 20-30% on inventory unwinds before end demand changes, and equipment orders lead chip revenue by 3-6 months. Practitioner comparisons (Morningstar, Harding Loevner, AJ Bell) all use Cisco 1999-2000 as the reference case for Nvidia.

### Cited Findings
- Greenwood, Shleifer & You (NBER 2017): "sharp price increases predict a substantially heightened probability of a crash"; after a 100% two-year run-up, slightly more than half of US industries (1926-2014) crash within two years, with a similar proportion internationally (1985-2014); volatility, turnover, issuance and the run-up's price path forecast the crash and future returns — [NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23191); [HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=53447)
- Semiconductor cycle structure: four phases (upcycle acceleration with rapid capex expansion; peak/early downturn with peaking revenue and large capacity plans; downcycle; trough with inventory normalization); downcycle every 3-4 years driven by inelastic supply and fab decisions made years ahead — [Basis Report](https://www.basisreport.com/resources/semiconductors-stock-analysis-blueprint); [Investing.com Academy](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/how-to-analyze-semiconductor-stocks/)
- "AI capex represents a flow of dollars moving from a small group of hyperscalers to a wide set of suppliers, with semiconductor equipment orders often leading the broader chip market by 3-6 months" — [Stocks Down Under](https://stocksdownunder.com/which-semiconductor-stocks-survive-the-cycle/)
- Inventory-driven revenue declines of 20-30% "before end demand has moved at all" — [Investing.com Academy](https://www.investing.com/academy/trading/how-to-analyze-semiconductor-stocks/)
- Practitioner Cisco-Nvidia comparisons exist at Morningstar, Harding Loevner and AJ Bell; Cisco's peak P/E of 201 on $19B revenue is the standard benchmark — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/nvidia-2023-vs-cisco-1999-will-history-repeat); [Harding Loevner](https://www.hardingloevner.com/insights/nvidia-and-the-cautionary-tale-of-cisco-systems/); [AJ Bell](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/group/news/2025-more-bubbly-1999). Contrast: Nvidia's forward P/E fell from 52x (June 2023) to ~17-25x (2026) as earnings outran price — [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/nvidias-p-e-ratio-current-levels-historical-trends-and-outlook) (via memo)
- Goldman Sachs Research argues a "dashboard approach is most appropriate" for timing the AI capex slowdown, compiling leading indicators including semiconductor-equipment imports into Taiwan and South Korea and relevant PMIs — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/global-investment-is-forecast-to-exceed-1-trillion-in-2026)

- A SOX drawdown/recovery history table exists but could not be fetched — [QuantFlowLab](https://quantflowlab.com/semiconductor-cycle-history/)

### Inferences
- Combining the industry-level crash base rate (>50% after a 100% run-up) with the supplier-specific record (KQ2: 6 of 6 named winners fell >70%), a reasonable working prior for a picks-and-shovels name that is up 3x+ in this cycle is: >50% probability of a 40%+ drawdown within two years of the top, and conditional on the capex cycle actually turning, a 70-90% drawdown is the modal outcome for the highest-beta suppliers.

### Inferences
- Mapping the 2000-01 sequence (KQ3) onto today: stage (1) leader stalls — *present* (Nvidia four months without a high; SOX -6% day); stage (2) supplier inventory build — *not visible*; the sourced signals point the opposite way (Nvidia supply-constrained, $279B of purchase commitments, memory 20% under-supplied); stage (3) second-tier vendors cut forecasts — *partial* (Marvell, Broadcom guide misses, but on mix not demand); stage (4) largest customer warns — *absent* (all hyperscalers raised or held); stage (5)-(7) — absent. On the 2000 template the cycle is at the "stocks top before fundamentals" stage, roughly where Cisco was in spring-summer 2000, if it is topping at all.
- The financing indicators are the most 2000-like: 93% of OCF going to capex, equity raises, negative FCF at Oracle, secured debt at neoclouds. In Greenwood-Shleifer-You terms, issuance is one of the attributes that raises crash probability.
- The indicator that would most cleanly mark the turn, based on history, is a hyperscaler 2027 capex guide *below* current consensus ($935B-$1.01T) at the Oct-Nov 2026 prints, followed by an inventory build at SMCI/Dell/Celestica and a Nvidia purchase-commitment decline. Second-order tells to watch: memory contract prices rolling over (2/3 of Sandisk's growth is price), EML/optics lead times shortening, and neocloud lease cancellations.
- Given Sandisk +45x and Micron +9x in 12 months, the memory complex is where a 2001-style 70-90% drawdown is most probable on a turn; Credo/Astera/Lumentum carry betas near 3 (memo §6) and would fall 40-60% in a risk-off tape even without a fundamental break.
